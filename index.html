How it works

Prices

Players are valued on how good they are compared with others at their position: this season's stats blended with last season's, then updated after every game. Each standard deviation of performance roughly doubles the price, so superstars cost many times more than role players.

A game only moves a price by how much it beat or missed that player's usual level. A star playing like a star stays put; a role player's breakout game jumps. Teams start from last season's record (regressed toward .500) and shift as this season's wins, losses and point differential come in.

Market hype is a slow random drift on top of everything, like a real market's mood, so nothing is a sure thing.

Star premium: the top 10% of players in each league (ranked at the start of the season) carry up to a 4\xD7 premium, so household names are the expensive ones in every sport. Teams are the blue chips: a .500 team is about $1,000 a share.

Injuries cut a player's price (Day-to-day \u22124%, Questionable \u22125%, Out \u221220%, IR \u221228%) and weigh on their team. News is scored for sentiment and nudges price for a few days. Your trades move the price too, and you pay a 0.35% spread.

Dividends

Teams pay after every win, more for upsets: a coin-flip win pays NBA 0.25%, NFL 1.5%, MLB 0.12% of the share price; a heavy favorite's win pays less and an underdog's more. Players pay after games that beat their own usual level, so stars and role players yield about the same. Milestone games (40 points, 3 homers, 5 TD passes\u2026) pay a 0.4% special dividend.

You must own the shares before the game starts. Index funds pass through the dividends of everything they hold.

Options

Each contract covers 100 shares. A call pays off if the price finishes above the strike; a put if it finishes below. Contracts expire Fridays at 4pm New York time and settle in cash automatically.

Prices mostly jump when a game's box score comes in, so premiums are built from game risk: every game before expiry counts as a possible move sized from that player's or team's own recent games, plus a little for news and injuries. Premiums rise into game day and drop once the game is played. You can buy to open and sell to close any time before expiry. The most you can lose is what you paid.

Moment cards & Marketplace

Moment cards are real plays from recent games: home runs, dunks, touchdowns and monster box-score nights. Each has a play rating (late, close and go-ahead plays rate higher), which sets its rarity from Common to Iconic.

A card only works on the player who made the play. Turn it on while you own his shares and it boosts your earnings from him: Dividend cards raise his dividends, Game Day cards pay a bonus when his games lift the price, and Shields give back part of the loss when they drop it. Each of his games uses one charge.

In the Marketplace, other collectors auction cards. Bids are max bids: if yours is the highest when time runs out, you win and pay just above the next bidder. You can also buy now, or list your own cards.

Orders

Market orders fill now, in shares or dollars (fractional shares). Limit orders fill only at your price or better. Stop orders become market orders once the price crosses your stop, so they work as a stop-loss. Recurring buys invest a fixed amount daily or weekly. Orders are checked every few seconds while the app is open, and when you reopen it.
